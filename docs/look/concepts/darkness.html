How dark should the night be?

Same painterly scene at three darkness levels. The amber circle is the lantern's light: inside it you see full colour; outside it you only see what the moon shows you. Gameplay needs you to still read cliff edges, water and hazards outside the light.

1 · Moonlit

Bright, cool moonlight. You can see the whole island, the cliff edges and the sea clearly. The lantern is a warm accent rather than a lifeline.

Play: most readable and least scary. The lantern matters less visually, though it still drives reveals and moths.

Chosen

2 · Balanced (recommended)

Deep blue night. Outside the lantern you see shapes and silhouettes, and the moon picks out cliff edges, the shoreline and water in pale blue rims. Inside the lantern's light, everything is full warm colour.

Play: the lantern feels essential and the world feels big and quiet, but hazards stay readable through moonlit edges.

3 · Deep night

Near black. Outside the lantern's light there's almost nothing, just a dim moon and a faint shoreline. Pure horror-adjacent tension.

Play: the most atmospheric and the most frustrating. Cliff edges, water and Shades become hard to read, and the beacon compass and HUD have to carry navigation. Low-brightness monitors suffer.

Note

Whichever you pick, a brightness calibration screen (in the Phase E settings) lets players adjust it. This choice sets the default look and what the art is tuned for.
