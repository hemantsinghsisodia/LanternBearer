Which art direction should Lantern Keeper commit to?

Each panel shows the same moment: the keeper with the lantern on a shore at night, an unlit beacon, the sea and a far islet. Compare the style, not the composition. All three are rough illustrations of a direction, not final art.

Chosen

A · Stylized painterly night (recommended)

Soft gradients, deep ink-blue and violet, simple shapes read as silhouettes. The lantern is the only warm light, with a strong amber glow and moonlit edges. Texture is painted or kept flat instead of photographic.

Feels like: Alba, A Short Hike at night, Firewatch's palette.

Fit: matches the low-poly keeper and our shader-driven look, and is cheapest to make consistent. It reads clearly at night.

B · Grounded realism

Photographic rock, grass and water textures, physically based lighting, heavy fog and darkness. Detail comes from materials rather than shape and colour.

Feels like: Dear Esther, Kona, realistic survival games at night.

Fit: builds on the Poly Haven assets, but the keeper, creatures, beacons and UI would all need realistic remakes. That's the most work, and the hardest to keep readable in darkness.

C · Low-poly graphic

Hard-edged facets, flat-shaded triangles, a limited palette and crisp geometric light shapes. The look is clean and readable rather than atmospheric.

Feels like: Alto's Odyssey at night, Monument Valley, many low-poly indies.

Fit: matches the current keeper's faceting and is quick to make, but it looks less atmospheric. Darkness and fog are harder to make feel mysterious with hard facets, and the style is common.

How to answer

Click the direction you like, then reply in the terminal. Mixing them is fine, e.g. "A, but with the stronger fog from B".
