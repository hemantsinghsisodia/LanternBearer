What should a beacon look like, unlit and lit?

Beacons are what you light to win, so they need to be readable from far away when unlit (a cold silhouette, plus a moonlit edge), and to give a payoff moment when lit. The lit beacon also shows its safe ring, about 8 m across. Today it's a cylinder base with a flame.

A · Stone brazier

A carved stone bowl on a pillar. Unlit, it's cold ash with a moonlit rim. Lit, a tall flame with rising embers and a warm glowing ring on the ground. It's clear and classic, but generic: "fire bowl" is used everywhere.

Chosen

B · Keeper's lamp-post (recommended)

A big version of your storm lantern on a dry-stone cairn: the same peaked roof, iron frame and four panes. Unlit, it has dark glass and a moonlit roof edge. Lit, the panes blaze and throw four soft beams, with a warm ground ring. It ties your lantern, the beacons and the Phase 3 lighthouse into one family.

C · Standing-stone cairn

A stacked-stone cairn under a stone gateway, with a flame in its heart. Ancient and ritual-feeling, with good silhouettes. It moves the fiction toward old magic rather than keepers and lighthouses.

Lighting moment (any choice)

When you light a beacon: a quick flare, the light ramps up over about 1 s, a burst of embers or sparks, a deep "whoomp" sound, and the safe ring blooms outward on the ground. Unlit beacons are also what lightning flashes highlight on Island 4.
